DELIBERATION ARCHITECTURE

How the Science and Security Board Sets the Clock

The Doomsday Clock is not a meteorological forecast or a mathematical index; it is an expert consensus assessment formulated by world-leading authorities.

01 / GOVERNANCE Expert Panel

Who Sets the Clock?

The decision is made biannually by the Science and Security Board (SASB) of the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists. The board comprises internationally renowned physicists, climate scientists, treaty negotiators, and cybersecurity scholars, acting in close consultation with the Board of Sponsors, which includes 9 Nobel laureates.

02 / CRITERIA Structural Shifts

What Triggers Movement?

The Clock is not adjusted in response to daily news spikes or short-term rhetoric. Instead, the Board assesses whether the world is safer or more dangerous compared to the previous year, examining long-term institutional agreements, treaty ratifications or expirations, and irreversible physical thresholds.

03 / CONSENSUS Unanimous Deliberation

How is Consensus Reached?

Board members convene in Chicago and Washington D.C. every November. Working groups present classified briefings, peer-reviewed climate data, and satellite imagery across each pillar. A consensus reading is forged through rigorous cross-examination before the public announcement in late January.

04 / SYMBOLISM Midnight Meaning

What Does Midnight Represent?

Midnight represents catastrophe: the irreversible disruption of global civilization through nuclear exchange, runaway climate feedback loops, weaponized pandemics, or unaligned superintelligent autonomy. It serves as both an urgent warning and an imperative call to civic action.
