CHRONOLOGICAL EPOCHS

Historical Era Comparative Analysis

Four fundamental geopolitical eras defined the movement of the minute hand over 79 years.

1947 – 1953 Dawn of Atomic Terror

Early Atomic Age

The Clock debuted at 7 minutes to midnight on the June 1947 Bulletin cover. By 1953, the US and USSR tested thermonuclear hydrogen bombs, precipitating a rush to 2 minutes to midnight.

Range: 7m 00s → 2m 00s (Delta: −300s)
1960 – 1984 Brinkmanship & Détente

Cold War Standoff

A turbulent oscillation between treaty-driven hope (Partial Test Ban 1963, NPT 1968, SALT I 1972 pushing the clock back to 12m) and terrifying brinkmanship (Cuban Missile Crisis, Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, Pershing II deployment down to 3m).

Range: 2m 00s ↔ 12m 00s (Delta: ±600s)
1991 – 1998 The Golden Thaw

Post-Soviet Peace Dividend

The end of the Cold War, signing of the Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty (START I), and the formal dissolution of the Soviet Union propelled the Clock to its all-time safest record: 17 minutes to midnight (1991).

Range: 17m 00s → 9m 00s (Record: 1,020s)
2020 – 2026 Existential Convergence

The Seconds Era

For the first time in history, the Bulletin abandoned minutes for seconds: 100 seconds in 2020, 90 seconds in 2023, and 85 seconds in 2026. Driven by the Russia-Ukraine war, climate tipping points, and autonomous AI weapons.

Range: 100s → 90s → 85s (All-time closest)
